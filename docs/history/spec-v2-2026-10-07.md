## F1 Dashboard – Spec

A three-screen Formula One display, a gift for the user. Version 2 – updated 7 October 2026. This is a living document: change it whenever a decision changes.

### 1. What we are building and why

Three small touch screens, mounted side by side in matching black frames, showing live Formula One races. It should look like a piece of artwork in an expensive house, not a hobby gadget. A guest should be able to walk up, notice it is interactive, and play with it.

Who it is for: the user, an F1 fan. Who builds it: the maker owns the look and feel; the developer does the hands-on technical build (uploads to the boards).

Rule: no paid services, no official F1 logo (we use our own AF1 badge), no copied real commentary.

### 2. The three screens

All three screens are landscape (480 wide x 320 tall). Left to right:

- Left – Standings board. The running order, tyres, and the top three up close.

- Middle – Map board (the hero). The track with the cars racing round it.

- Right – Drama board. Everything else that happens in the race, plus all the sound.

Each screen has its own board and its own program. Each board gets the live data itself over wifi. The boards also talk to each other directly (ESP-NOW), so a tap on one screen can change another.

Look shared by all three: dark background (deep navy or black), one accent colour used sparingly, our own AF1 badge, same broadcast-style detail on all three.

#### The whole race weekend

The dashboard goes live for practice and qualifying as well as the race. Each session shows what it is about:

- Race: the full works, with the map as the hero.

- Qualifying: the timing and the order shuffling take centre stage, plus the hot-lap view (section 3).

- Practice: a calm, simple version. Shows it is practice, the times, and who is quickest.

### 3. Middle screen – Map board

Between races: shows the next race's map (one of the 23 finished season maps, version 4, already signed off). Round badge top corner, flag other top corner, track name top middle, speed-colour track, corner numbers, sector lines in purple / green / yellow, streets or contours faintly behind, key along the bottom, time and date bottom right.

During a race:

- The board picks the right track by itself from the live data.

- Each car is a small car icon in team colour with its number. Small tyre icon next to each car.

- Thin strip at the edge showing the running order, plus the current lap (e.g. lap 12 of 58).

- Top three get pulsing rings: gold (1st), silver (2nd), electric blue (3rd). Rings swap cars on a pass. Pulse speeds up as the gap closes. Silver and blue only switch on when the gap is under 2 seconds.

- Any close battle anywhere on the grid gets a flashing ring in team colours. Closest battles flash fastest. No more than 4 or 5 at once.

- Crash: a red ring ripples out from the crashed car's spot.

- Safety car: bright yellow marker, bigger than the cars, strobing like a beacon.

- Small 3-letter code (e.g. VER) next to the top three only. No faces on this screen.

- Extra layers that can be switched on: starting grid at the start-finish line (where each car started), rain, DRS zones glowing, gap line from the leader to second, racing line, 3-letter codes on every car.

- When cars bunch up and overlap, labels nudge outward. (Solve properly when coding.)

#### Pinned cars and halos

- Up to four cars can be pinned at once, shared however people like. First in, first served.

- Pin a car by tapping it on the map, or by tapping the driver on the standings board.

- Each pinned car gets a flashing halo in a colour picked from a swatch of four. Halos flash out of step with each other and with the podium rings, so every ring stays easy to tell apart.

- Swatch colours: pink, white, purple and orange (to be confirmed). Later nicety: grey out a colour already in use.

- Pinning only adds the halo. The map does not zoom unless someone asks it to.

#### Follow and zoom

- Tap a car and a small panel pops up with two buttons: "Follow 30 seconds" (zooms in, follows the car, then goes back to the full track) and "Stay on" (follows until stopped). The colour swatch is on the same panel.

- While following, a clear "Unfollow" button shows on screen.

- Only one car can be followed at a time. The user's car comes first; if the user hasn't picked one, it follows the maker's.

#### Qualifying hot lap

- The map keeps tracking the same car through the session, zoomed or not.

- A car on a flying lap pulses, so you know to watch it.

- This screen shows the whole track. As the car runs its lap, each sector lights up in timing colours: purple for fastest of anyone, green for the driver's personal best, yellow for slower.

- The zoomed-in close-up of the car goes on the drama board, and the standings board shows the timing (to be confirmed).

- During a hot lap the static sector lines are hidden so they don't clash with the live colours.

- If two cars are on a hot lap at once, follow the one higher up the order.

- No slow-motion replays: the live data has car positions only, no video.

### 4. Left screen – Standings board

Default view: every driver in race order, with their tyre and how many laps that set has done. The current lap shows at the top.

Feature view: every so often it breaks away to the top three, one driver at a time, each filling the whole screen: a big photo of the driver and their car, their position, and the gap. Then back to the full list.

- Kept tight on purpose: gaps, weather, fastest lap, pit stops and crashes all live on the drama board.

- Tyre age is worked out by us (current lap minus the lap the stint started, plus one). OpenF1's own tyre-age figure is often wrong for used sets.

- Fuel load is left off. Teams never release it, and we won't show a guess.

- In qualifying this board shows the timing: times tumbling, the order shuffling, provisional pole, and the knockout drop zone.

- Driver photos: 21 of 22 downloaded from the official F1 site, 319 x 319, to be cropped to head and shoulders. Arvid Lindblad has no photo yet, so use a plain silhouette with his number 41 on it until F1 posts one.

- Exact layout: worked out by eye once laid out.

### 5. Right screen – Drama board

Carries everything that is not standings: current lap, gaps, weather and track conditions, fastest lap, pit stops, safety car, flags and crashes.

- Incident card: when there's a crash, the board shows the driver's face and car with what happened, in the same style as the standings feature view.

- In qualifying, this board shows the zoomed-in close-up of the car on its hot lap.

- This board holds the speaker and amplifier and plays ALL the sound for the dashboard.

### 6. Sound

All sound is finished and saved in the F1 Dashboard folder (Sounds and Commentary folders). Real recordings only – no made-up synth sounds (except the start lights).

- Effects: five-light race start, pit stop rattle gun, F1 engine, two crashes (single car and pile-up) – the board takes turns between them.

- Commentator "Ryan" (computer voice, British, excited): 31 lines. Crash at turn 1 to 20 (board plays the right corner from the live data), safety car out / coming / in, taken the lead, brilliant overtake, yellow flags, green flag, lights out, chequered flag, fastest lap, into the pits.

- Ryan only speaks for big moments, so the room stays calm most of the time. No siren – Ryan's "safety car" call does that job.

- Volume: on-screen slider plus one-tap mute.

- Store sounds as MP3, not WAV, to save space.

### 7. Between races – attract mode

When nothing is running, all three screens work together as one display and never go dark. The sequence loops round:

- Championship top three (about 2 minutes): leader on the left, 2nd in the middle, 3rd on the right. Each screen flips from the driver's photo, to their car, to a stats card.

- Badge swipe: the AF1 badge sweeps across all three screens as if they were one long screen, on a white background, like a wipe between scenes.

- Next race: track, date, time and expected weather, spread across the screens.

- Next three races: left is the next race, middle the one after, right the one after that. Each has its own live countdown in New Zealand time, showing days, hours, minutes and seconds.

- Everything except the top three runs 30 seconds to a minute. Exact timings set when building.

- The map board is the "conductor" and tells the other two when to change, using ESP-NOW (direct board-to-board, no router needed).

- When a session starts, all three snap into live mode.

- The badge: "AF1", with the user's name spelled out underneath when there's room. Used everywhere in place of the official F1 logo.

### 8. Touch – what you can tap

Map board

- Tap a corner to see its name (e.g. "Copse, turn 9").

- Tap the track to zoom into a section.

- Tap a car to see who it is and their position, with the pin, colour swatch and follow buttons.

- A small menu icon in a corner opens the layer switches (streets/contours, speed colours, sectors, corner numbers, rain, DRS zones, driver codes, racing line). The board remembers the choice, even after power-off.

- Volume slider and one-tap mute.

Standings board

- Tap a driver for their card: photo, stats, and how their race is going.

- Tap a driver to pin them, so their car gets a halo on the map.

- Tap a driver for their race story: where they started, where they are now, places gained or lost.

- Tap a tyre to see its age.

- Tap the top of the list to flip between race order and championship points.

Drama board

- Tap a crash to replay its incident card.

- Tap a pit stop for its time, how it ranked, and how it compares with the fastest stop of the day.

- Tap the fastest lap for who holds it, the time, which lap, and who is closing in on it.

- Tap the weather for track temperature, air temperature, and rain coming.

- Tap "race so far" for a summary of the big moments (pits, crashes, lead changes), then tap any moment to open it up.

- Tap a driver named in the drama to jump to their card.

- Tap the safety car to see why it's out and how many laps it has been out.

- Switch between "live" and "quiet" mode. Quiet is calmer and shows the next thing to watch for.

- Tap to make Ryan say something about what's happening right now.

- Tap for the biggest mover: most places gained and most lost.

- Tap for "what to watch": a battle brewing.

Secret: a hidden tap on the AF1 badge shows a personal message for the user.

### 9. Hardware and data

- Three LCDwiki ES3C35P boards: ESP32-S3, 16 MB flash, 8 MB PSRAM, 3.5 inch 320 x 480 IPS touch screen, ST77922 screen chip.

- Screen code comes from LCDwiki's own demo (no common library supports this chip).

- Arduino IDE 1.8.19. Settings: ESP32S3 Dev Module, USB CDC On Boot = Enabled, PSRAM = OPI PSRAM.

- Power: one 4-port USB wall charger, three short cables.

- Sound: MAX98357 amplifier and small speaker on the drama board (the developer to check if the speaker in the box is good enough).

- Live data: OpenF1 (free). Maps: our own finished season maps.

- Space: maps + program about 8 MB, all sound about 1.5 MB – fits easily in 16 MB.

### 11. Rules for building

Always: test each piece on the real board before moving on; save new work to the F1 Dashboard folder; build in small steps.

Ask the maker first: anything that changes the look; adding new sounds or features; spending money.

Never: paid services; the official F1 logo; copied real commentary; synth sounds in place of real ones.

Build order: wifi + live data first → map with test dots → map with real cars → standings → drama board and sound → attract mode and sync → touch features → practice and qualifying views.

### 12. How we'll know it's done – and what's still open

Done when:

- During a real race, all three screens update on their own within a few seconds of the live data, with no one touching anything.

- The map board loads the right track by itself.

- Ryan calls a crash at the right corner.

- Practice and qualifying each show their own view.

- Between races, all three screens run the attract sequence together and never go dark.

- Every tap listed in section 8 works.

- The user loves it.
